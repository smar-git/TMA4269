Step 6: Is our observed difference significant ?

The p-value tells us something of the probability that our observation just occurred by chance.

The smaller the p-value the more convinced we are that our data do not fit the null hypothesis and must be evidence of something else.

Working example

Step 6: Is our observed difference significant ?

Often, we compare our p-value to a predefined significance level such as 5%.

We then accept a 5% chance of rejecting the null hypothesis even if it is true.

If the p-value is less than 5% we say that our result is statistically significant at the 5% level.

P-hacking

If we have a dataset and do many different tests / comparisons, then just by chance we will always find some small p-values.

P-hacking, data dredging, data snooping, cherry picking:

The misuse of data analysis to find patterns that can be presented as statistically significant

P-hacking: solutions?

Multiple testing correction:

If we would normally consider p < 0.05 to be a significant result, then if we do M tests we have to consider p < 0.05/M.

E.g. 100 tests, p < 0.0005 implies significance

P-hacking: solutions?

Be honest / transparent:

  • Write out a plan beforehand and stick to it

  • If the study is exploratory (searching for patterns, generating hypotheses) then state that
